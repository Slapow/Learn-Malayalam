# Vocabulary editing notes

English prompts use one primary sense for the Central Travancore family and
conversation setting of this course. Related senses belong in usage notes rather
than a list of slash-separated answers. Necessary grammatical distinctions remain,
including inclusive/exclusive “we” and casual/respectful “you”. Complete sentences
retain their punctuation and are not shortened to an arbitrary word count.

For this course, **Chettan** teaches “older brother” and **Achayan** teaches
“husband”. Achayan is also used for other older male relatives and in-laws; that
usage varies by family. Ammachi and Appachan retain the intended grandmother and
grandfather senses without repeating the region in the English prompt. These are
selected teaching senses, not claims that kinship usage is identical in every family.

The review also corrects script/transliteration mismatches and overly specific
translations: mesha (table), pani (fever), vayaruvedana (stomach ache), kadala
(chickpeas), moru (buttermilk), and madhuram (sweetness).

References checked for kinship usage:

- [Rodney F. Moag, *Malayalam: A University Course and Reference Grammar*](https://utw11087.utweb.utexas.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2021/06/Moag-Malayalam-CompleteTextbook.pdf),
  Lesson 12, printed page 181, discusses Christian wives addressing husbands as
  Achayan. The glossary gives Chettan as “older brother”.
- [Malayalam Wiktionary: അമ്മച്ചി](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/അമ്മച്ചി)
  records the grandmother sense.

The Python generators and `curriculum_additions.py` are the editable vocabulary
sources. The five Kotlin phrase files and `curriculum_inventory.tsv` must agree
with them. `python -m unittest test_curriculum.py` checks that agreement and the
key corrected meanings, as well as existing curriculum/progress invariants.

This editorial review is not a native-speaker certification of every dialectal
or figurative expression in the course.
